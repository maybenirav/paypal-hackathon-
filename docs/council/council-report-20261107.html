The Contrarian strongest per peer review

The flaw that sinks you if you leave it: you can't show what you're selling.

  • The LTV winner runs on seeded data, and seeded data makes any engine pick the winner you planted.
  • A sharp judge will ask what in the demo is real. The answer is one sandbox subscription, verified separately.

Your core loop contradicts itself:

  • Rollout: the AI suggests arms that change interval and trial, but PayPal only rolls out arms whose interval and trial match the baseline. Limit the agent to price-only arms, or show it rejecting arms PayPal can't roll out. That rejection is your best "AI meets real constraints" moment.
  • Thompson sampling: it needs fast rewards, but LTV takes months. Splitting by conversion and then judging on LTV is muddled statistics.
  • Save Agent: "pause" doesn't exist in PayPal, and the agent is deterministic rules. Calling rules an "agent" at an AI hackathon invites mockery.
  • Market: small merchants don't have the traffic to reach significance.

Rot judges will see:

  • In-memory storage leaves an empty app after a restart. Seed data on startup.
  • The unsourced 80% claim.
  • A sprawl of features on a thin frontend.
  • Sponsor prizes with no sponsor code installed.

What to do:

The First Principles Thinker

You're asking the wrong question. "Is PlanLab winnable?" assumes the experiment engine is the product.

  • Small merchants don't test prices because they lack sign-ups and LTV takes months, not because they're missing a Bayesian engine.
  • A Beta-Binomial posterior on 40 sign-ups is a nice-looking way to say "we don't know yet". A sharp judge catches this in about 30 seconds.

What works at n=1 is the Save Agent. Churn happens one customer at a time, and the result shows up in days.

Rebuild from the ground up:

  • Reframe as "a PayPal subscription copilot that saves churning customers and suggests price changes when you have enough data".
  • Lock experiments until the merchant has enough sign-ups. Saying so out loud shows judges you understand the statistics.
  • Build the video on real PayPal objects: create a subscription, cancel it, the Save Agent steps in, a real plan change happens. Mark anything simulated.
  • Delete one stats engine.
  • Use AG Grid for the save-decision table, and Render with SQLite.
  • Drop Bryntum, KERNEL, the AG Studio claim and the 80% claim, which argues against your own core feature.
The Expansionist biggest blind spot per peer review

You're pitching the weak half of your product. Your hidden asset is that you're PayPal-native, and PayPal sees millions of merchants.

  • Pooled priors: seed each arm's Beta prior from a synthetic, labelled "PayPal benchmark" of similar merchants. Demo line: "Alone you'd need 4,000 sign-ups; with PayPal's network prior, 300." Experiment memory becomes cross-merchant learning.
  • Promote the Save Agent: reframe as a revenue copilot. The Save Agent keeps revenue today, and experiments grow it over time.
  • Agentic commerce: expose both agents as tools through PayPal Agent Toolkit or MCP.
  • Sponsors: AG Grid and Render are cheap. Cut Bryntum, KERNEL and APIMatic.
The Outsider

  • Two products. That says you couldn't choose.
  • "AI that suggests prices", which any chatbot can do.
  • Sponsor logos without a reason.

What lands instantly: the Save Agent.
